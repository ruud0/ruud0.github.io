Research Assistant, The Pennsylvania State University

Aug to Dec 2025; extended 2026
  • Built the Python dynamic-scheduler model, run visualizations, and report for Prof. Steven Landry's study comparing traffic signal control with reservation-based scheduling at urban intersections.
  • Extended the study independently in 2026: rebuilt it as a from-scratch Python microsimulation of a six-signal grid with pedestrians as agents, then tested fixed-time, semi-actuated, and reservation control at five demand levels, eight seeds each (120 runs, 241,938 vehicles).
  • Found that reservation scheduling cut stops per vehicle 4.5 to 5.1 times, but under heavy demand raised mean pedestrian wait from 14.3 s to 34.6 s while signal control held near 13 s. The original study could not show this because it did not model pedestrians.
  • Added an intersection occupancy check that exposed a safety gap in the initially favored reservation controller.
